### Agenda & Discussion

1. **Data collection strategy.** The group confirmed the decision to use synthetic data generation rather than real data collection. Rationale: (a) known ground truth enables rigorous benchmarking of data-cleaning strategies, (b) no ethical/privacy concerns with publishing synthetic data in the public repository, (c) the parameterised generator can encode the exact hypothesised effects for pipeline validation.

2. **Generator scope.** Confirmed that the proof-of-concept generator targets RS3 exclusively, justified by its maximal structural complexity and richest data-type coverage. The other three research stories (RS1 note-formatting, RS2 planning tools, RS4 background noise) are deferred to further work.

3. **Output format agreement.** The group agreed on tidy-format CSV output (Wickham, 2014) with four tables:
   - `rs3_participants.csv` (80 rows) — demographic and grouping variables
   - `rs3_trials.csv` (~42,000 rows) — trial-level learning, review, and test data
   - `rs3_sessions.csv` (480 rows) — session-level aggregates
   - `rs3_tests.csv` (320 rows) — test-occasion summary scores
   - `rs3_data_dictionary.csv` — codebook for all columns

4. **Data privacy reminder.** The group acknowledged the syllabus requirement that real data from individuals must **not** be published in the public repository. Since only synthetic data is used, there are no privacy concerns for this project.

### Participants
- Group members + AI assistant

### Discussion Summary

1. **Generator implementation approach.** Discussed implementing `0202.py` as a modular generator with separate functions per table. The generator should:
   - Accept `--seed` and `--n` (participants per condition) CLI arguments
   - Output tidy CSV files with consistent row counts
   - Preserve the ground-truth dataset before injecting imperfections
   - Include ground-truth flags (`is_bogus`, `is_missing_true`) for detection-method evaluation

2. **Distribution sampling details.** Finalised the sampling code approach:
   - Ex-Gaussian RTs: sum of normal(μ, σ) and exponential(τ) — `numpy.random.normal(mu, sigma, n) + numpy.random.exponential(tau, n)`
   - Logistic accuracy: `scipy.special.expit(linear_predictor)` → Bernoulli
   - Truncated normal JOL: `scipy.stats.truncnorm.rvs(a, b, loc, scale)` with [0, 100] bounds
   - Negative binomial trials-to-criterion: `numpy.random.negative_binomial(r, p)` + 1 (zero-truncated)
   - Per-participant random intercepts: `numpy.random.normal(0, 0.3, n_participants)`

3. **Missing-data injection logic.** Discussed implementing each mechanism:
   - **MCAR:** Independent random deletion — `numpy.random.random(n) < p_missing`
   - **MAR (Day 97):** Logistic probability depends on observed digit-span scores — `logit⁻¹(α₀ + α₁ · span_z)`
   - **MNAR (Day 37):** Logistic probability depends on the unobserved true retention score — `logit⁻¹(γ₀ + γ₁ · true_score_z)`
   - **Attrition:** Monotone dropout process — once a participant drops out at occasion t, they are absent for all t' > t

4. **Careless responding injection.** Following Meade & Craig (2012):
   - 5% random JOL values: replace with uniform(0, 100)
   - 3% straight-lining: all JOL values identical within a participant
   - 0.2% implausibly-fast RTs: replace with uniform(50, 150) ms
   - 0.5% implausibly-slow RTs: replace with uniform(10,000, 30,000) ms
