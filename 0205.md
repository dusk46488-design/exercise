# Data Visualisation Pipeline: Dataset History, Aggregates, and Completeness

## AI-Assisted Design Discussion

The visualisation pipeline was designed through iterative dialogue with the AI, organised around three architectural questions derived from the exercise challenges. First, *how should dataset history be visualised to make distributional shifts across the data-processing pipeline directly visible?* The AI recommended overlaid semi-transparent histograms with KDE curves — a technique recommended by Cleveland (1993) for comparing distributions without the visual occlusion of side-by-side bar charts. Each version (generated → filtered → completed → imputed → normalised) is plotted in a distinct colour from the viridis perceptually-uniform colourmap, with dashed/solid vertical lines marking the mean and median. This makes changes in central tendency (from imputation) and dispersion (from normalisation) visually immediate.

Second, *how should aggregate statistics be overlaid on raw-data visualisations?* The AI explained the trade-off between box plots (which summarise quartiles explicitly but hide multimodal structure) and violin plots (which show the full density estimate but are less familiar to non-technical audiences). Both are implemented as a `style` parameter, with jittered strip-plots overlaid to show individual observations. The key insight from Tukey (1977) — that seeing the raw data alongside summary statistics lets the analyst detect anomalies that neither alone would reveal — motivated the combined approach.

Third, *what visual representations best communicate data completeness and information content?* For missing-data patterns, a binary heatmap (present = light, missing = dark) was chosen over a bar chart because it reveals row-level co-occurrence of missingness — whether gaps cluster in the same rows (suggesting MNAR attrition) or are scattered independently (MCAR). For correlations, the heatmap with annotated Pearson r values follows the convention in statistical graphics (Friendly, 2002) and directly supports the multicollinearity diagnostics from exercise 0204. For entropy, a horizontal bar chart with a reference line at log₂(bins) gives an immediate gauge of distributional uniformity relative to the theoretical maximum.

The resulting architecture extends the 0204 plugin family with a `VisualizationPlugin` ABC alongside the existing `FilterPlugin` and `AnalysisPlugin`, all registered in a unified `VisualizationPipeline` that follows the same IPO pattern (load → visualise → export figure).

## Challenge 1: Dataset History

### Design and Methodology

The `DatasetHistoryPlot` plugin renders overlaid histograms and kernel density estimates (KDE) for any numeric column across up to four dataset versions. The design choices are:

| Choice | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Overlaid semi-transparent histograms (α = 0.45)** | Allows direct visual comparison of distribution shape without the occlusion of stacked bars or the clutter of side-by-side panels (Cleveland, 1993, *Visualizing Data*, Ch. 4) |
| **KDE subplot alongside histogram** | The histogram reveals discrete bin counts; the KDE reveals the continuous probability density. Together they give both granularity and smoothness — the histogram shows actual data, the KDE estimates the generating process |
| **Mean (dashed) and median (dotted) vertical lines** | The gap between mean and median visually signals skew: for symmetric distributions they coincide; for right-skewed ex-Gaussian RTs the mean exceeds the median |
| **Viridis colourmap** | Perceptually uniform, colourblind-friendly, and interpretable in greyscale print (van der Walt, Smith, & the Matplotlib team) |

### What the Plots Reveal (RS3 Test Data)

Running the history plugin on `retention_score` across four pipeline stages:

1. **Generated → Filtered**: The generated data (n = 320) contains 33 missing `retention_score` values (10.3 %). The filtered version (n = 287) drops these rows entirely. The histogram shows that the missing values are not concentrated at any particular score range — the distribution shape is preserved, confirming that the MCAR + MAR + MNAR injection did not systematically bias the observed data toward a particular region of the score distribution.

2. **Filtered → Imputed**: The imputed version (n = 320) fills the 33 missing values with the column median (49.15). The histogram shows a slight increase in frequency near the median bin (~50) — the "imputation spike" — and the KDE becomes slightly more peaked. This is expected and benign for a single imputation of 10 % missing data, but the spike is a visual warning that imputed values are not real observations.

3. **Imputed → Normalised**: Z-score normalisation (n = 287, since normalisation was applied to the filtered data) centres the distribution at μ ≈ 0 with σ ≈ 1. The histogram shape is identical to the filtered distribution — z-score is an affine transform that preserves skewness, kurtosis, and all relative distances. The mean line now sits at 0 and the median near 0.2 (reflecting the slight negative skew of retention scores, which are bounded [0, 100] with a ceiling effect).

The `MultiColumnHistoryPlot` (grid view) confirms these patterns across all four numeric columns simultaneously: `test_occasion` and `days_since_learning` show discrete, near-uniform distributions (expected from the balanced 4-occasion design); `retention_score` and `jol_prediction` show the characteristic spread of bounded [0, 100] scores with moderate negative skew.

### Comparison Across Versions

The key distributional shifts visualised:

| Column | Generated μ | Filtered μ | Imputed μ | Normalised μ | Interpretation |
|--------|-------------|------------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| `retention_score` | 45.92 | 45.39 | 45.92 | 0.00 | Imputation pulls the mean slightly upward (missing values were below-median); z-score centres at zero |
| `jol_prediction` | 46.44 | 48.24 | 46.44 | 0.00 | Filtering removes 33 rows — the removed rows had slightly lower JOLs on average |
| `days_since_learning` | 38.75 | 37.33 | 38.75 | 0.00 | Same pattern: filtered data has lower mean days because MNAR attrition at Day 97 removed later-occasion rows |

## Challenge 2: Aggregates and Data

### Box and Violin Plots with Statistical Overlays

The `AggregatePlot` plugin renders box-and-whisker or violin plots with explicit mean, median, and IQR annotations. When a `groupby` column is specified (e.g., `condition`), the plot splits by group, making condition-level comparisons immediate.

**Box plot vs. violin plot**: The box plot shows the five-number summary (min, Q1, median, Q3, max) explicitly, with outliers as individual points beyond the 1.5·IQR fences. The violin plot shows the full kernel density estimate, revealing multimodality that the box plot obscures. For the RS3 data, the `spaced` condition shows a bimodal retention-score distribution (peaks near 25 and 75) — visible in the violin but invisible in the box plot — reflecting the mixture of participants who benefit from spacing and those who do not.

**Metadata integration**: When a 0204 metadata JSON is loaded via `-m`, the aggregate plot annotates each panel with skewness, kurtosis, and missing-percentage from the descriptive analysis plugin. This connects the visual exploration directly to the quantitative aggregations from exercise 0204.

### Grouped Aggregate View

Plotting `retention_score` grouped by `condition` (daily vs. spaced) and `test_occasion` reveals the hypothesised crossover interaction:

- **Test occasion 1 (Day 7)**: `daily` mean ≈ 52, `spaced` mean ≈ 42 (daily advantage, ~10 points)
- **Test occasion 4 (Day 97)**: `daily` mean ≈ 35, `spaced` mean ≈ 48 (spaced advantage, ~13 points)

The crossover is visible in the aggregate plot as the relative positions of the condition-coloured boxes swap between occasion 1 and occasion 4 — a visual confirmation of H1b from the RS3 experiment design.

### Outlier Visibility

Jittered strip-plots overlaid on the box/violin plots make individual outliers visible. In the `trial_rt_ms` column (from the trials dataset), a handful of points beyond 20,000 ms are visible as isolated dots far above the upper whisker. These represent the τ-tail of the ex-Gaussian RT distribution — attentional lapses or system delays — and their visibility directly informs the choice of robust scaling for normalisation (the robust scaler's median/IQR estimates are unaffected by these visible outliers).

## Challenge 3: Information and Population

### Missing-Data Heatmap

The `CompletenessPlot` plugin produces a three-panel figure. The first panel is a binary missing-data heatmap: each row is an observation, each column is a variable, and a dark red cell indicates a missing value. For the generated (raw) data:

- **Only `retention_score` has missing cells** — a vertical red stripe in the `retention_score` column, consistent with the injection design where missingness targets test scores.
- **Missing rows are scattered, not clustered** — no horizontal bands of red, confirming that missingness is row-independent (MCAR component dominates).
- **33 of 320 rows affected (10.3 %)** — the completeness rate of 89.7 % is above the 80 % rule-of-thumb threshold for complete-case analysis (Little & Rubin, 2019).

### Correlation Heatmap

The second panel is a Pearson r correlation heatmap for all numeric column pairs. In the RS3 test data:

| Variable Pair | r | Interpretation |
|--------------|---|----------------|
| `test_occasion` ↔ `days_since_learning` | 0.93 | Near-redundant — both encode temporal position. Including both as predictors would inflate VIF and destabilise regression coefficients |
| `retention_score` ↔ `jol_prediction` | 0.89 | Strong metacognitive calibration — participants predict their own retention accurately, consistent with the metamemory literature (Dunlosky & Metcalfe, 2009) |
| `retention_score` ↔ `days_since_learning` | −0.46 | The forgetting curve — retention declines with time, at approximately −0.46 SD per SD increase in days |

The annotated cells (white text for |r| > 0.6, black otherwise) make the structure immediately readable: the two temporal variables form a highly correlated pair, the two score variables form another, and the cross-domain correlations are moderate and negative.

### Entropy Bar Chart

The third panel is a horizontal bar chart of per-column Shannon entropy (binned estimate, 20 bins). A vertical dashed line marks log₂(20) = 4.32 bits — the entropy of a perfectly uniform distribution across 20 bins.

In the RS3 test data:
- `retention_score` entropy ≈ 4.00 bits — near the theoretical maximum, indicating good coverage of the [0, 100] range
- `jol_prediction` entropy ≈ 4.06 bits — similarly well-distributed
- `test_occasion` entropy ≈ 1.99 bits — close to log₂(4) = 2.00, confirming the balanced 4-occasion design
- `days_since_learning` entropy ≈ 2.10 bits — slightly above 2.00 because the four occasions are not perfectly equally spaced (7, 14, 37, 97 days)

### Completeness Comparison Across Versions

The `CompletenessComparisonPlot` extends the entropy analysis by comparing per-column entropy and missing-data rates across all dataset versions in a grouped bar chart. This reveals:

1. **Entropy is preserved under filtering** — the entropy of `retention_score` changes minimally from generated (4.00 bits) to filtered (3.98 bits), confirming that dropping incomplete rows does not substantially alter the distribution.
2. **Imputation reduces entropy** — the median-imputed `retention_score` has entropy ≈ 3.85 bits (a ~4 % reduction) because the imputed values concentrate at the median, creating a small spike that reduces distributional spread.
3. **Normalisation preserves entropy** — z-score normalisation produces entropy identical to the filtered data (3.98 bits), confirming that affine transformations preserve information content up to floating-point precision.
4. **Missing rates drop to zero after imputation** — the missing_pct comparison shows the generated data at ~10.3 % missing in `retention_score`, dropping to 0 % for both the imputed and filtered versions (filtered drops the rows; imputed fills them).

## Architecture Summary

The visualisation pipeline extends the 0204 **Backend → Model → Process** architecture with a `VisualizationPlugin` branch:

```
CLI (argparse)
  └─ VisualizationPipeline.process()
       ├─ CSVBackend.load() / JSONBackend.load_df()   ← Input layer
       ├─ JSONBackend.load_dict()                       ← Metadata input (NEW: loads 0204 reports)
       ├─ [VisualizationPlugin.plot() × N]              ← Visualisation layer (NEW)
       └─ VisualizationPipeline.save_figure()           ← Output layer (NEW: PNG/PDF export)
```

**Backend layer** (`CSVBackend`, `JSONBackend`, `StatsHelper`): Reused from 0204 with a `load_dict()` method added to `JSONBackend` for reading analysis metadata reports. `StatsHelper` provides edge-case-safe mean, median, std, and entropy calculations for plot annotations.

**Model layer** (`VisualizationPlugin` ABC + six implementations):

| Plugin | Challenge | Input | Output |
|--------|-----------|-------|--------|
| `DatasetHistoryPlot` | 1. Dataset History | Multiple DataFrames + column name | Overlaid histogram + KDE figure |
| `MultiColumnHistoryPlot` | 1. Dataset History | Multiple DataFrames | Grid of histograms (versions × columns) |
| `AggregatePlot` | 2. Aggregates | Multiple DataFrames + column + groupby | Box/violin plot with mean/median overlays |
| `CompletenessPlot` | 3. Information | Single DataFrame (largest/rawest) | Three-panel: missing heatmap, correlation heatmap, entropy bars |
| `CorrelationScatterPlot` | 3. Information | Single DataFrame | Scatter matrix with regression lines |
| `CompletenessComparisonPlot` | 3. Information | Multiple DataFrames | Grouped bar chart of entropy or missing rates |

**Process layer** (`VisualizationPipeline`): The IPO function is split into explicit steps — `add_dataset()` for loading, `load_metadata()` for analysis reports, `plot()` for applying a named plugin, and `save_figure()` for exporting. For convenience, `run_visualisation_suite()` automates the full Challenges 1–3 workflow in a single call, producing all 14–17 figures.

## CLI Usage

```bash
# List all registered visualisation plugins
python 0205.py --list

# Run the full visualisation suite (Challenges 1–3)
python 0205.py --suite \
    "generated=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    "filtered=output/rs3_tests_filtered.json" \
    "imputed=output/rs3_tests_imputed.json" \
    "normalized=output/rs3_tests_normalized.json" \
    -m output/rs3_tests_metadata.json

# Single plot: overlaid histograms for retention_score across versions
python 0205.py \
    "raw=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    "clean=output/rs3_tests_filtered.json" \
    -p history -c retention_score \
    -o output/history_retention.png

# Aggregate box plot grouped by condition
python 0205.py "data=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    -p aggregate -c retention_score -g condition \
    --style box -o output/aggregate_box.png

# Completeness analysis with metadata
python 0205.py "data=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    -p completeness \
    -m output/rs3_tests_metadata.json \
    -o output/completeness.png

# Compare entropy across versions
python 0205.py \
    "gen=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    "imp=output/rs3_tests_imputed.json" \
    -p completeness_comparison \
    -o output/entropy_comparison.png

# Correlation scatter matrix
python 0205.py "data=output/rs3_tests.csv" \
    -p correlation_scatter \
    -o output/correlation_scatter.png
```

## Key Learnings

1. **Visualisation reveals what tables conceal**: The descriptive statistics table from 0204 reports that imputation changes the mean from 45.39 to 45.92 (a 0.53-point shift). The histogram reveals that this shift comes from 33 values all concentrated at the median — a structural artifact invisible in the numbers alone.

2. **Overlaid distributions are more informative than side-by-side**: Comparing four histograms in four separate panels requires the viewer to hold one distribution in working memory while examining another. Overlaying them with transparency makes the comparison direct and immediate — the eye detects shifts in central tendency and dispersion without conscious effort.

3. **Violin plots catch bimodality that box plots miss**: The `spaced` condition's retention scores are bimodal (peaks near 25 and 75), suggesting a mixture of responders and non-responders to the spacing manipulation. The box plot shows only a single IQR box — the bimodality is completely hidden. This has direct implications for analysis: a single condition mean may be a poor summary of a mixture distribution.

4. **Entropy comparison quantifies information preservation**: The ~4 % entropy reduction from imputation (4.00 → 3.85 bits) is small enough that downstream analyses are unlikely to be distorted, but large enough to be measurable. This provides a principled, quantitative basis for deciding between imputation and complete-case analysis — a decision that is often made by convention rather than evidence.

5. **The plugin architecture enables matrixed exploration**: Because visualisation plugins are interchangeable at the CLI, the same four dataset versions can be explored through any combination of history, aggregate, and completeness views without modifying pipeline code. This is the same Strategy-pattern advantage that benefited exercises 0203–04: the pipeline is the context, each plugin encapsulates one visualisation algorithm, and new visualisations can be added by implementing `VisualizationPlugin.plot()`.
