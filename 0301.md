### Agenda & Discussion

1. **Experimental design review.** The group reviewed Research Story 3 (RS3) from the Study Efficiency Optimization project: a 2 (review method: spaced repetition vs. daily massed review) × 4 (testing occasion: Days 7, 14, 37, 97) mixed factorial design. Confirmed that the primary dependent variable is retention score (0–100), with secondary DVs including judgment of learning (JOL, 0–100), trial-level reaction time (ms), and trials-to-criterion counts.

2. **Hypothesis formalisation.** The group agreed on three hypotheses:
   - **H1:** Spaced repetition produces significantly higher retention at Day 37 and Day 97 compared to daily review.
   - **H1a:** The spaced group shows a shallower forgetting slope (smaller decay per log-day).
   - **H1b:** A crossover interaction — daily group outperforms at Day 7, but spaced group overtakes by Day 37 and maintains superiority through Day 97.

3. **Scope decision.** The group decided to scope the proof-of-concept to RS3 only, following the pilot-study rationale of Leon et al. (2011). RS3 was selected because it spans the richest data-type coverage (binary, continuous, longitudinal, Likert, between-subjects factor) and has the highest structural complexity among the four research stories.

### Discussion Summary

1. **Logistic mixed-effects model specification.** Discussed the retention-score generation model:

   $$P(\text{correct}_{ict}) = \text{logit}^{-1}(\beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{spaced} + \beta_2 \cdot \log(\text{days}) + \beta_3 \cdot \text{spaced} \times \log(\text{days}) + u_i)$$

   Agreed on parameter values: β₀ = 2.2 (~90% at Day 7 for daily), β₁ = −0.4 (spaced lower at Day 7), β₂ = −1.2 (daily forgetting slope), β₃ = +0.8 (spaced forgetting is shallower), and uᵢ ~ N(0, 0.3) as a per-participant random intercept. This parameterisation encodes the hypothesised crossover: daily advantage at early time points reversing to spaced advantage by Day 37.

2. **Distributional assumptions.** Reviewed the behavioural-science literature to justify distribution choices:
   - Trial-level RTs: Ex-Gaussian(μ, σ, τ) — the τ parameter separately models the slow attentional-lapse tail (Wagenmakers & Brown, 2007)
   - Recall accuracy: Bernoulli trials with logistic probability (standard for binary outcomes)
   - JOL ratings: Truncated normal centred on true score + condition-specific overconfidence bias (+5 for daily, 0 for spaced)
   - Trials-to-criterion: Zero-truncated negative binomial with overdispersion φ = 1.5

3. **Pipeline architecture design.** Discussed the layered Backend → Model → Process architecture with plugin registry following the Strategy design pattern (Gamma et al., 1994). Each processing concern (filtering, normalisation, aggregation, visualisation) is encapsulated in an abstract base class (ABC) with a single-method contract. Concrete implementations are registered and selectable via CLI flags.

4. **Data imperfection plan.** Discussed injecting realistic imperfections following established taxonomies:
   - Missing data: MCAR, MAR, MNAR mechanisms (Little & Rubin, 2019)
   - Careless responding: random values, straight-lining, implausible RTs (Meade & Craig, 2012)
   - Longitudinal attrition: monotone process, ~25% total dropout
